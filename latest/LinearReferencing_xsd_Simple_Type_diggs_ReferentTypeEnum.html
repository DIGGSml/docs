Annotations
 
Referent types for geotechnical, geoenvironmental and geophysical linear features:
common reference points in drilling, sampling and survey operations.
Diagram
Type restriction of string
Facets
enumeration ground surface 
The natural or existing ground surface at the location of the feature. The
usual reference for boreholes, CPT soundings, test pits and other subsurface
investigations.
enumeration kelly bushing 
The kelly bushing on a drilling rig, at the top of the kelly drive system.
Typically the reference for measured depth in rotary drilling. Rotary
bushing is the preferred term.
enumeration rotary bushing 
The bushing seated in the rotary table through which the kelly or drill
string passes. Its elevation (RKB, rotary kelly bushing) is a common datum
for measuring drilled depths and is typically a few feet above the drill
floor elevation. Preferred over kelly bushing.
enumeration rotary table 
The rotating platform of a drilling rig through which the drill string
passes. An alternative reference for measured depth.
enumeration crown valve 
The valve assembly at the crown of the derrick or mast, at or near the crown
block. A depth datum in drilling operations, at one of the highest fixed
points of the rig.
enumeration derrick floor 
The derrick floor or rig floor, the main working platform of the drilling
rig. Sometimes used instead of the kelly bushing or rotary table.
enumeration mudline 
The river or lakebed surface in over-water drilling: the sediment-water
interface in non-marine aquatic environments.
enumeration sea floor 
The sea floor surface, used in marine geophysical and geotechnical surveys.
Typically the reference for measured depth in subsea wells and offshore
boreholes.
enumeration mean sea level 
Mean sea level, a vertical datum in marine and coastal environments: the
arithmetic mean of hourly water elevations observed over a National Tidal
Datum Epoch (19 years).
enumeration lowest astronomical tide 
The lowest tide level over a National Tidal Datum Epoch (19 years). A
low-water datum in some coastal applications.
enumeration mean higher high water 
A tidal datum: the average of the higher high water height of each tidal day
observed over a National Tidal Datum Epoch (19 years). Applicable in areas
with mixed tidal regimes.
enumeration mean high water 
A tidal datum: the average of all the high water heights observed over a
National Tidal Datum Epoch (19 years).
enumeration mean lower low water 
A tidal datum: the average of the lower low water height of each tidal day
observed over a National Tidal Datum Epoch (19 years). The common datum for
nautical charts in the United States.
enumeration mean low water 
A tidal datum: the average of all the low water heights observed over a
National Tidal Datum Epoch (19 years).
enumeration mean tide level 
A tidal datum: the arithmetic mean of mean high water and mean low water,
the same as half-tide level.
enumeration top of casing 
The top of the protective casing of a borehole or well. A reference for
depth measurements in monitoring wells and environmental boreholes.
enumeration bottom of casing 
The base of the protective casing of a borehole or well, where cased hole
changes to open hole.
enumeration casing flange 
A flange affixed to the top of the casing string, used to attach production
equipment. A common reference in oil and gas wells.
enumeration kickoff point 
The point at which a borehole deviates from vertical in directional
drilling. An important reference for deviated well paths and horizontal
drilling.
enumeration top of hole 
The top of a drilled hole; generally the same as the ground surface unless
the hole is drilled within an excavation or from a platform.
enumeration bottom of hole 
The terminal end of a borehole or well at maximum penetration: the total
depth (TD) of the hole.
enumeration top of core 
The defined top of a core sample. Used for positions within core samples
that are handled separately from the parent borehole, and a common anchor
point for linear referencing within a core.
enumeration bottom of core 
The defined base of a core sample. Used for core recovery calculations and
sample referencing; often better defined than the top of core when slough is
present at the top of the cored interval. A common anchor point for
bottom-up core logging.
enumeration line start 
The starting point of a geophysical trackline or survey transect,
corresponding to the first coordinate of the linear extent. A common
reference for distance along survey lines.
enumeration line end 
The ending point of a geophysical trackline or survey transect,
corresponding to the last coordinate of the linear extent.
enumeration intersection 
The point where this linear feature crosses another linear feature. Common
in grid-based geophysical surveys, where perpendicular survey lines create a
network of tie points.
enumeration benchmark 
A surveyed benchmark used as the elevation reference for the feature: a
monumented point with known coordinates in a geodetic reference system.
