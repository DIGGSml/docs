Element diggs:SurfaceBoundedSolidType / diggs:footprint
Namespace http://diggsml.org/schemas/3
Annotations
 
The lateral extent of the volume: one planar polygon
(diggs:PlanarSurface) whose boundary is the trace of the vertical
sides, or several disjoint polygons (diggs:MultiSurface, each member
expected to be a diggs:PlanarSurface) when the plan-view extent is
itself disconnected, for instance a geologic unit present as
separate unconnected patches. Unlike topSurface and bottomSurface,
which are each one continuous surface, the footprint may be a
diggs:MultiSurface, because each component is one planar region and
walls are erected on each component's own boundary; a MultiSurface
footprint over one pair of continuous bounding surfaces yields a
separate body for each member. Each component's exterior ring is its
outer wall; each interior ring is a hole through the body and
generates its own wall. Components shall not overlap in plan view.
The footprint is normally horizontal and authored as a plan-view
polygon, but the content model does not require it. Elevation
carried by the footprint's coordinates is not the volume's top or
bottom: the footprint supplies (x, y) only, and the bounding
surfaces supply elevation.

Element diggs:SurfaceBoundedSolidType / diggs:topSurface
Namespace http://diggsml.org/schemas/3
Annotations
 
The upper bounding surface of the volume; for a geologic body, the
top of the unit. It is one continuous surface: a
diggs:PlanarSurface, a multi-patch diggs:MultiPlanarSurface or
diggs:CompositeSurface (whose patches or members share boundaries, a
step being a vertical member), or a diggs:TriangulatedSurface mesh;
not a diggs:MultiSurface. It shall extend to or beyond the footprint
over the footprint's whole area. The portion outside the footprint
is not part of the solid, which allows one authored surface to serve
several adjacent bodies.

Element diggs:SurfaceBoundedSolidType / diggs:bottomSurface
Namespace http://diggsml.org/schemas/3
Annotations
 
The lower bounding surface of the volume; for a geologic body, the
base of the unit, which is commonly the top of the unit below and
may be the same surface object cited twice by reference. It admits
the same surfaces as topSurface and is subject to the same coverage
requirement. It may touch or cross topSurface: where it lies at or
above the top the body pinches out and that part of the footprint is
empty; see diggs:SurfaceBoundedSolid.
