Element diggs:SeismicHazardParametersType / diggs:seismicSiteClass
Namespace http://diggsml.org/schemas/3
Annotations
 
The site classification the site coefficients were read against, as
investigated: the ground as found, before any improvement. The same
element name is used on diggs:RITreatedAreaType and
diggs:DesignRITreatedAreaType so that one enumeration governs all
three, but they state different facts about the same scale: this one
is what the investigation found and the hazard lookup used, the
design-side one is what the design predicts improvement will
achieve, and the treated-area one is what was achieved. A project
that improves the ground enough to change its class states all three
and they will not agree; that is the finding, not an error. Values
come from the enumerated list, or "Other: xx", where xx is a
user-defined term whose meaning differs from every listed value (not
a synonym of one).

Element diggs:SeismicHazardParametersType / diggs:peakGroundAcceleration
Namespace http://diggsml.org/schemas/3
Annotations
 
a_g: the design peak ground acceleration adopted as the seismic
hazard premise, read from a national hazard map or a site-specific
hazard study for the return period implied by the design working
life and consequence class. It is not derivable from the values
above and must not be synthesized from them: in ASCE 7 and the codes
derived from it, a site-adjusted peak ground acceleration comes from
a separately mapped peak ground acceleration and its own site
coefficient, and the coefficients above apply to spectral ordinates,
not to it. Where a source states spectral values but no peak ground
acceleration, leave this absent. It is a
diggs:LinearAccelerationMeasureType rather than a dimensionless
value because peak ground acceleration is quoted as a fraction of
standard gravity at least as often as in m/s2; the mandatory uom,
"gn" for the former and "m/s2" for the latter, keeps 0.3 g from
being read as 0.3 m/s2. A measured strong-motion record is a
different thing in a different domain and is not modelled here.
