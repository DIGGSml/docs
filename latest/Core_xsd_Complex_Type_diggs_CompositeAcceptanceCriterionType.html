Element diggs:CompositeAcceptanceCriterionType / diggs:logicalOperator
Namespace http://diggsml.org/schemas/3
Annotations
 
How the member criteria combine. One operator applies across all
members; write a mixed expression such as "A and (B or C)" as nested
composites, the inner one declared first and cited by the outer
through criterionRef. There is no "not": negate a comparison by
using the complementary operator on the member criterion. Values
come from the enumerated list.

Facets
enumeration and 
Every member criterion must be satisfied, for example a drilling refusal
rule such as penetration rate below X and torque above Y, which is met only
when both hold together.
enumeration or 
At least one member criterion must be satisfied, for example a termination
rule that stops at design depth or on reaching a specified resistance,
whichever occurs first.

Element diggs:CompositeAcceptanceCriterionType / diggs:criterionRef
Namespace http://diggsml.org/schemas/3
Annotations
 
A member criterion: a diggs:PerformanceCriterion, a
diggs:DesignConformanceCriterion or, where the expression nests,
another diggs:CompositeAcceptanceCriterion. A composite has at least
two members. A member is cited rather than contained, because a
criterion is cited from elsewhere (by a diggs:DesignVerification or
a report) and may take part in more than one composite; declare it
before the composite that cites it. The target is a criterion, not
any other program specification, which the validation chain checks.
Given by reference only.
