Annotations
 
The design of one geosynthetic reinforcement layer within a load transfer platform:
product identity, stiffness and strength (each meaningless without the strain level
and elapsed time they were taken at), the reduction factors applied to reach a
long-term design value, the tension the layer must carry by load path, anchorage,
allowable strain, and where the layer sits within the platform. Generic design
machinery, not specific to rigid inclusions: geosynthetic-reinforced load transfer
platforms are standard practice for column-supported embankments generally,
including stone columns and driven piles. The as-built counterpart,
diggs:GeosyntheticType, is declared in the rigid inclusion schema, so design and
as-built for this concept are in different files. Tensile stiffness (J) is an
isochronous secant modulus, defined only once a strain level and elapsed time are
stated, and long-term design strength depends on design life and the
reduction-factor chain in the same way; both are also direction-dependent, since
machine and cross-machine directions differ substantially in most geogrids. Hence
atStrain, atTime and atDesignLife accompany the stiffness and strength, and
direction is its own field. Repeat this object once per layer: its presence states
that basal reinforcement is used and the number of repeats is the layer count, so
neither is stated as a separate value.
