Element diggs:SurfaceBoundedSolid
Namespace http://diggsml.org/schemas/3
Annotations
 
A volume of earth material whose sides are vertical, encoded as a top surface, a
bottom surface and a footprint that fixes its lateral extent: one planar region, or
several disjoint planar regions when the plan-view extent is itself disconnected
(see diggs:PlanarSurfaceOrMultiSurfacePropertyType). It is intended for geologic
bodies, such as a stratigraphic unit, a geotechnical layer or a zone of treated
ground, whose top and bottom vary in relief but whose lateral boundary is vertical
everywhere. It states only the independent geometry and leaves the side walls to be
computed, which for a many-vertex footprint or a finely triangulated top surface is
a large reduction in instance size compared with a diggs:Solid, which needs a
diggs:Shell enumerating every bounding polygon, including one side polygon per
footprint edge. Use diggs:Solid for a volume whose sides are not vertical.
Construction: the solid is the region between topSurface and bottomSurface, bounded
laterally by vertical walls on the footprint's boundary or, when the footprint is a
diggs:MultiSurface, on the boundary of each member independently, which gives a
separate disjoint body per member; together they make up the one
SurfaceBoundedSolid. The footprint decides only where a wall stands in plan view,
never how tall it is. A wall lies in the vertical plane through its footprint edge,
and its upper and lower edges follow the profiles of the top and bottom surfaces
along that edge. A wall therefore changes slope wherever a surface does, and where a
surface has a vertical member crossing the edge (a step) the wall steps with it, so
that the solid is closed; where a surface is one plane, or linear along the whole
edge, the wall reduces to one planar quadrilateral. The vertices of the top and
bottom surfaces need not coincide with each other or with the footprint's. A
footprint interior ring (a hole through the body, or through one member) generates
its own wall. Footprint members shall not overlap in plan view. Coverage: the top
and bottom surfaces shall each extend to or beyond the footprint over its entire
area. A body whose surface stops short of its footprint is not a valid
diggs:SurfaceBoundedSolid, and no rendering is defined for it. The XSD cannot
express this geometric condition; check it by instance-level geometry validation of
the footprint against the extent of each bounding surface. Bounding surfaces:
topSurface and bottomSurface are each one continuous surface, a diggs:PlanarSurface,
diggs:CompositeSurface, diggs:MultiPlanarSurface or diggs:TriangulatedSurface (see
diggs:BoundingSurfacePropertyType), not a diggs:MultiSurface, whose members have no
assumed adjacency. A body present as several disjoint pieces is expressed with a
diggs:MultiSurface footprint over continuous top and bottom surfaces: each footprint
member is walled and capped independently. The members of a diggs:CompositeSurface
and the patches of a diggs:MultiPlanarSurface shall share boundaries, adjacent
members meeting along a common boundary curve in three dimensions, so a step is
represented by a vertical member or patch, never by a gap. Neither surface need be
planar, single-patch or monotonic. Crossing surfaces: the two surfaces may touch or
cross. The solid exists only where topSurface lies above bottomSurface; where the
top is at or below the bottom the body has no thickness and that part of the
footprint is empty. A body may pinch out within its footprint, and the surviving
regions are separate parts of the one solid. A consumer should treat the empty
regions as void, with the parts of the walls that would stand on them, rather than
render an inverted volume. No GML counterpart: GML represents a solid only by
explicit boundary representation (gml:Solid bounded by gml:Shell), so this is a
DIGGS construct with nothing in GML to align to. It substitutes diggs:AbstractVolume
and its type derives from diggs:AbstractVolumeType, which derives from
gml:AbstractSolidType, so a schema-aware GML consumer sees a member of the solid
family, and only a DIGGS-aware consumer needs to know how to expand it.
