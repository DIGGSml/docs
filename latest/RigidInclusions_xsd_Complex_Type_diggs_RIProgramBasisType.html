Element diggs:RIProgramBasisType / diggs:analysisApproach
Namespace http://diggsml.org/schemas/3
Annotations
 
The class of calculation the design was arrived at by: a published
analytical method, numerical (finite-element or finite-difference)
analysis, engineering judgment or precedent, or pre-production or
proof load testing. It is distinct from designMethod: this states
which class of calculation was used, and designMethod states which
published method, and applies only when this element is analytical
method, since numerical analysis, personal experience and load
testing select no named method. State this first. It is not named
designApproach because the vocabulary of safetyFormat already uses
"design approach" for the Eurocode 7 DA2 and DA3 partial-factor
combinations, a different concept (which factors to apply, not how
the calculation was performed). Values come from the enumerated
list, or "Other: xx", where xx is a user-defined term whose meaning
differs from every listed value (not a synonym of one).

Element diggs:RIProgramBasisType / diggs:designMethod
Namespace http://diggsml.org/schemas/3
Annotations
 
The specific published method the design was calculated by, where
analysisApproach is analytical method: FHWA beam, FHWA
strain-compatibility, BS 8006-1, EBGEO, CUR226, Nordic or other.
Leave it absent where analysisApproach names numerical analysis,
personal experience or load testing, none of which selects a method
from this list. The dictionary also keeps its earlier, broader
vocabulary (unit-cell method, arching, finite element analysis,
load-displacement-compatibility method), which names a mechanism
family rather than a method. Use the DIGGS dictionary
designMethod.xml for the list of values.

Element diggs:RIProgramBasisType / diggs:exposureClassification
Namespace http://diggsml.org/schemas/3
Annotations
 
The ground aggressivity or exposure classification governing the
durability provisions the design specifies for the concrete or grout
and for any embedded steel reinforcement, derived from the sulfate,
chloride and pH results that a diggs:LabChemicalTest reports. It is
something the design was prepared from, an environmental condition
established before design, so it is on a basis rather than on
diggs:DesignRITreatedAreaType. The companion durability inputs
designWorkingLife and consequenceClass are inherited from
diggs:AbstractProgramBasisType. Ground aggressivity has two
independent axes: chemical attack on the concrete or grout, graded
from sulfate, magnesium, chloride and acidity; and electrical
corrosivity to embedded steel, graded from soil resistivity. A site
routinely carries a rating on each, from different authorities, so
repeat for each classification. The codeSpace is mandatory: several
schemes use the same severity words on completely different criteria
(an ACI 318 S3, a BS 8500 AC-5, a Palmer resistivity rating and a
client standard may all read "very severe"), so a bare severity word
is uninterpretable. Use the DIGGS dictionary
exposureClassification.xml, whose codes are scoped to their
authority: EN 206 XA classes, ACI 318 S and C categories, BS 8500
ACEC classes and a client standard.
